Track packing — collision-only vs. group-coherent

Packing only matters where cues overlap in time (echo / harmony / ad-libs). Same cues below, packed two ways. Hover a group to see how its cues are distributed — that's the whole question.

Strategy 1Collision-only (fewest rows)lowest free row, ignores group

1: the most compact result — but a single group can land on different rows across the song (hover Harmony: its two cues split across rows). The eye can't track a group as one thread.

Strategy 2Group-coherent runseach group keeps one row; compatible groups share Recommend

2: each group stays on one row (hover Harmony — now contiguous), and groups that never overlap share a row to stay compact. Reads as phrases; usually the same row count, occasionally one more.

▍ Verdict

Go group-coherent. Pure collision optimises a number nobody's counting (rows) at the cost of the thing you scan for — a group reading as one continuous line. Coherent packing keeps each group on a single row, still merges non-overlapping groups so it stays tight, and only adds a row when overlap genuinely forces it.

Tie-breakers to confirm: (a) order rows by each group's first start (lead on top)? (b) when a group self-overlaps (true simultaneous words), allow it a second row — or is that the cue-merge case instead? (c) keep Grouped view as the explicit per-event extreme when even coherent Track gets too tall.
